A bed size name can create a false sense of certainty.

A buyer may request a Single, Double, Queen or King bed frame and assume that the size is immediately clear. In reality, the same name can refer to different mattress dimensions in different markets. A UK King is not a US King. A European 160 x 200 cm mattress is close to some market references, but it is not automatically interchangeable with a US Queen or a UK King.

For a consumer, this can result in an uncomfortable fit. For a B2B buyer, importer or private-label brand, it can affect the product drawing, frame structure, carton size, assembly instructions, SKU naming and the compatibility statement shown to customers.

The safest approach is simple: use the regional size name for communication, but use exact dimensions for product development and approval.

Core sizing ruleNever approve a B2B bed frame specification from the words Single, Double, Queen or King alone. Confirm the target country and the exact mattress width and length.

Why Bed Size Names Do Not Travel Well

Bed and mattress terminology developed differently across markets. The United States commonly uses inch-based names such as Twin, Full, Queen and King. The United Kingdom uses names such as Single, Small Double, Double and King, with dimensions that do not match the American system. Continental European buyers often work more directly with metric dimensions, such as 90 x 200 cm or 160 x 200 cm, although local naming and commonly stocked sizes can still vary by country and retailer.

This means that a size name is a market label, not a universal engineering specification.

Even when two sizes appear close, a difference of a few centimetres can affect whether the mattress sits correctly inside the frame, whether gaps are visible around the mattress, and whether the finished product can be described accurately in a retail listing.

Cross-market comparisons can be useful during an initial discussion, but the phrase closest equivalent should never be interpreted as automatically compatible.

Mattress Size Is Not the Same as Bed Frame Size

A mattress dimension describes the nominal sleeping surface. It does not automatically describe the finished footprint of the bed frame.

1. Nominal mattress size

The market reference used to describe the mattress width and length, such as US Queen 60 x 80 inches or UK King 150 x 200 cm.

2. Inner frame or mattress-fit dimensions

The usable internal area designed to receive and support the mattress. This dimension needs to consider the intended fit, the frame construction and the approved product specification.

3. Overall bed frame dimensions

The maximum finished width, length and height of the complete product, including side rails, headboard, footboard, legs, decorative details and any parts extending beyond the mattress area.

4. Packaged dimensions

The carton length, width and height after the frame has been disassembled and protected for shipment. Packaged dimensions are a separate logistics specification and cannot be calculated from the mattress size alone.

No universal frame-addition formula

Consumer guides sometimes estimate that a bed frame adds a few inches around a mattress. That estimate may help with room planning, but it is not precise enough for B2B product development. A platform frame, decorative headboard bed, bamboo frame, bunk bed or upholstered structure can have a very different finished footprint.

Why the Finished Frame Footprint Varies

Two bed frames designed for the same mattress can have different overall dimensions. The difference may come from:

  • the width and profile of the side rails;
  • whether the mattress sits inside the frame or on top of a platform;
  • the thickness and shape of the headboard and footboard;
  • decorative tubing, posts, caps or projecting details;
  • connection hardware and corner construction;
  • the position of legs and centre support;
  • the use of bamboo, metal, wood or upholstered components;
  • and the assembly and packaging strategy.

This is why a B2B specification should record both the intended mattress fit and the maximum finished product dimensions.

What Changes When a Bed Frame Changes Size

Changing a bed frame from one regional size to another is not always a simple scale adjustment. The full product system may need to be reviewed.

Structure and support

A wider or longer frame may affect the side rails, centre support, slat layout, leg position, connections and component strength. The approved structure should be reviewed for the intended size rather than copied automatically from another SKU.

Components and hardware

Longer rails, different support spans or revised headboard proportions can change the component list, hardware arrangement and assembly sequence.

Packaging and logistics

Size changes may affect carton length, component nesting, protection materials, shipping volume and handling. A larger mattress size does not always create a proportional increase in the flat-pack carton, because the disassembly strategy also matters.

Instructions and retail information

The size name, measurement unit, product dimensions, mattress compatibility statement, labels and assembly instructions should match the target market. A US retail listing should not inherit UK size terminology, and a European product should not rely only on an English size name without metric dimensions.

SKU planning

Every additional size can affect sampling, drawings, components, packaging artwork, inventory planning and product-page management. A B2B range should therefore include the sizes that serve the target market, not every size that exists globally.

Sample validation

Before production, the buyer and supplier should confirm the intended mattress fit, overall product dimensions, assembly result, support configuration and packaging direction for the approved SKU.

Market-Specific Considerations for B2B Buyers

United States

Use the exact inch-based mattress reference and confirm whether the requested range includes only Twin, Full, Queen and King or also Twin XL and California King. Dormitory and student-housing programs may place additional importance on Twin XL, while retail ranges may use different size combinations according to channel and category.

United Kingdom

Do not treat UK size names as direct translations of American names. Small Double is a meaningful UK category, while UK King is 150 x 200 cm rather than the much wider US King. Product labels, instructions and online listings should use UK terminology and metric dimensions consistently.

Continental Europe

Start with the exact dimensions and target country. Sizes such as 90 x 200, 140 x 200, 160 x 200 and 180 x 200 cm are widely recognized references, but local ranges and naming can vary. A dimension-led SKU description is usually clearer than assuming that Queen or King has one European meaning.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are US, UK and European bed sizes the same?

No. The names and nominal dimensions differ by market. Always confirm the target country and exact mattress width and length.

Does the mattress size equal the overall bed frame size?

No. Mattress size describes the nominal sleeping surface. Overall frame dimensions include the surrounding structure, headboard, footboard, legs and any projecting details.

Can a US Queen mattress fit a UK King bed frame?

The sizes are relatively close, but they are not identical. Compatibility should not be assumed without confirming the actual mattress dimensions and the frame's approved inner fit.

Is there one standard European King size?

Not in the same universal sense implied by the name. European products are more reliably discussed by exact metric dimensions and target country, such as 160 x 200 cm or 180 x 200 cm.

How much larger should a bed frame be than the mattress?

There is no single rule suitable for production specifications. The required difference depends on the frame design, construction, mattress-retention method and approved fit.

What information should a B2B buyer send first?

Start with the target market, sales channel, retail size name, exact mattress dimensions, product type and any requirements already fixed for height, structure, appearance, packaging or labelling.

Confirm the Market Before Confirming the Size

A successful bed frame program begins with a shared definition of size.

Regional names are useful for sales and communication, but exact dimensions are required for development. Mattress size, inner fit, overall frame dimensions and packaged dimensions should be treated as separate specifications.

When the target market and dimensional requirements are clear, the buyer and manufacturer can make better decisions about structure, components, packaging, labels and the final SKU range.
