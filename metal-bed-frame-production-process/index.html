How Our Metal Bed Frames Are Made

The sequence below outlines key stages in a typical metal bed frame program. Specific operations, checks and their order depend on the product design. Tube-end forming and robotic operations are used where required; they do not apply to every component or SKU.

01Requirement Review and Material Preparation02Laser Tube Cutting and Processing03Punching and Stamping04Tube End Forming Where Required05Welding and Structural Assembly06Grinding and Deburring07Surface Treatment and Powder Coating08Component Assembly and Trial Assembly09Packing Preparation and Carton Packing10Palletizing and Shipment Preparation
Step 01

Requirement Review and Material Preparation

Production starts with a review of the product type, size, structure, surface finish, packaging needs and target market. Steel tubes, plates, hardware, fasteners and coating materials are prepared and checked against the agreed specifications.

Why it matters

Confirming specifications early provides a consistent reference for component processing, assembly and packing.

Step 02

Laser Tube Cutting and Processing

Steel tubes are laser-cut and processed according to product drawings to create the required lengths and connection features. Separate hole-processing operations are used where the component design requires them.

Why it matters

Consistent component dimensions support fit during forming, welding and assembly.

Step 03

Punching and Stamping

Components are positioned in dedicated equipment to produce assembly holes and connection points. Press equipment and dies are used for the shapes or local forming features required by the design.

Why it matters

Hole locations and formed features need to match the parts they connect to during assembly.

Step 04

Tube End Forming Where Required

Some designs require specially formed tube ends. Where needed, tube ends are induction-heated and mechanically formed to create the profiles specified for the component. This operation is used for selected designs, not every bed frame SKU.

Why it matters

The tube-end profile must suit the intended connection or appearance of the component.

Step 05

Welding and Structural Assembly

Steel tubes, brackets and other structural components are positioned before welding. Manual and robotic welding are used according to the component and production requirements to create subassemblies for the bed frame.

Why it matters

Fixture positioning helps keep connection points aligned across repeated components. Welding and connection areas are reviewed as part of routine quality control.

Step 06

Grinding and Deburring

Formed and welded components are ground and deburred to remove sharp edges, welding residues and local surface irregularities before finishing.

Why it matters

Edge and surface preparation supports safer handling and a more even base for the subsequent finish.

Step 07

Surface Treatment and Powder Coating

Metal components are prepared for finishing, electrostatically powder coated and heat-cured according to the product requirements. The selected finish provides the specified colour and a protective surface.

Why it matters

Surface coverage and appearance need to remain consistent across the components supplied for a product.

Step 08

Component Assembly and Trial Assembly

Coated components are fitted with brackets, joints, fasteners and other required hardware. Mechanical fastening methods are selected to suit the component design.

Trial assembly and functional checks

Key components are trial-assembled before packing to review hole alignment, connection fit, structural stability and accessory completeness.

Why it matters

Checking component fit and accessory completeness helps identify assembly issues before packing.

Step 09

Packing Preparation and Carton Packing

Finished components are grouped by product configuration and prepared with the required accessories. Parts are arranged with protective materials in the appropriate cartons before sealing. Bundling and separation are used where needed to keep components organised and limit contact during handling.

Why it matters

Packing preparation needs to address component completeness, surface protection and the agreed carton configuration.

Step 10

Palletizing and Shipment Preparation

After carton packing and sealing, packed goods are organised for storage and shipment preparation. Robotic equipment handles and stacks cartons in the operations shown. Carton marks and shipment requirements are reviewed against the order.

Why it matters

Orderly carton handling supports the organisation of finished goods before dispatch.

Quality Checks Across the Process

Quality review is connected with several production stages. Routine checks focus on the agreed product specifications and the practical requirements for assembly, appearance and packing.

Dimensions and connection points

Review relevant component dimensions and hole positions against the product requirements.

Welding and structural connections

Review weld areas and important connections during production.

Surface appearance

Check coating coverage and visible surface condition.

Assembly fit and hardware

Review trial assembly, connection fit and the completeness of required hardware.

Packing and carton marks

Review protective materials, component grouping and carton identification.
